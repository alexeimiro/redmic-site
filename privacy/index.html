Redmic Privacy Policy

Effective November 25, 2026

Redmic is a voice keyboard for iPhone and Android made by OKNENOR L.L.C-FZ ("we"). This policy explains what Redmic collects, why, and the choices you have. Questions: miralex13@gmail.com.

What stays on your phone

  • Your transcripts (dictation history) and the recordings Redmic keeps so you can play them back. You choose how long they're kept (Settings › Local data storage).
  • What you type with the Redmic keyboard. The keyboard doesn't record or send keystrokes.
  • Your requests lists (Redmic Pro): what you asked for each day and what you checked off. They're deleted with the transcripts they come from.
  • The Requests reminder (Redmic Pro), if you turn it on: a notification your phone schedules itself. Nothing is sent anywhere to show it.
  • Your Redmic AI chats (Redmic Pro): your questions and the answers. They're deleted with the transcripts they're about, and you can delete them any time on Redmic AI's screen.

On Android, the Redmic keyboard is an input method, the kind of app Android lets you choose as your keyboard. The letters you type with it stay on your phone. It uses the microphone only while it's on screen and you've tapped its mic, and never in a password field. Your transcripts, recordings and Redmic AI chats are kept in Redmic's own storage on the phone, which other apps can't read, and are left out of Android's backup to your Google account.

What we collect and why

  • Your account: your email address (or the private relay address Sign in with Apple gives us), your name if Apple or Google passes it on, and an account ID. They let you sign in and keep your plan and library on every device, iPhone or Android.
  • Your recordings and their text, to write what you say: when you dictate, the audio goes from Redmic on your phone to our server and on to a speech service that turns it into text. A Word-to-Word dictation streams to Soniox while you speak, on iPhone and Android, so its text is ready as soon as you finish. The other writing modes, translations, and a Word-to-Word dictation Soniox can't answer quickly go to OpenAI when you finish. When the text needs tidying or translating, it goes to Anthropic, whose Claude model rewrites it. Redmic asks for your permission before the first recording is sent, and you can take it back in Settings › Data and privacy (Redmic then stops listening until you allow it again). Our server doesn't keep the audio or the text, unless you turn on Help improve Redmic (next). Soniox, OpenAI and Anthropic process them to return the result. Under their API terms none of them uses them to train its models. Soniox doesn't store what it hears live; OpenAI and Anthropic may keep it for a limited time to detect abuse (OpenAI for up to 30 days).
  • Your recordings, to train Redmic's own speech model (only if you turn on Help improve Redmic): Settings › Data and privacy › Help improve Redmic is off until you turn it on. While it's on, Redmic on your phone sends a copy of each dictation's recording to our server, which keeps it with your account ID and what describes it: whether you dictated in the app or with the keyboard, the language you chose to speak (not one a speech service detected), the writing mode, its length and the app version. No text is kept with it. We use these recordings only to build and test Redmic's own speech recognition, and people working for Redmic may listen to some of them to write down what was said. The copies stay on our server: we don't sell or share them, and we don't send them to Soniox, OpenAI or Anthropic. Your phone sends them only on Wi-Fi (or another network that isn't metered), never while you dictate. Turning the switch off deletes every recording kept for your account, and so does deleting your account.
  • The text of your dictations, to list your requests (Redmic Pro, only once you turn Requests on): when you open Requests, the text of the day's dictations it hasn't read yet, with earlier ones from the same day for context, goes from the app to our server and on to Anthropic, whose Claude model picks out what you asked for. The list comes back to your phone and stays there. Our server doesn't keep the text. You can turn Requests off on its screen.
  • Parts of your dictations and notes, to answer your questions (Redmic Pro, only once you start Redmic AI): when you ask Redmic AI something, the app searches your dictations and notes on your phone and sends only the parts that match, with their dates, together with your question, to our server and on to Anthropic, whose Claude model writes the answer. The answer comes back to your phone and is kept there with your chat. Our server doesn't keep the question, the parts or the answer. A question you say out loud is turned into text like a dictation, but it isn't added to your transcripts. You can turn Redmic AI off and delete your chats on its screen.
  • Text you paste into a note, to split it into paragraphs (on iPhone): when you paste a long block of text into a note, the app sends it to our server and on to Anthropic, whose Claude model picks where its paragraphs start. Only the line breaks change: the words come back as you pasted them, and you can undo it. Our server doesn't keep the text. This happens only while you're signed in and after you've allowed Redmic's AI services.
  • Your dictionary, snippets and notes: while you're signed in, they're saved to your account so they're on every device you use. They aren't shared with anyone.
  • Invites: if you join with a friend's invite code, we record whose code it was, so you each get your month of Redmic Pro. Your friend sees how many people joined with their code, not who. On Android, when you install Redmic from a friend's invite link, Google Play hands the code to Redmic, so you don't have to type it.
  • How much you dictate: the number of words in each dictation, to apply the free plan's weekly limit, and what each dictation cost us to serve: how many seconds of audio and how much text the AI services handled, and which service did it. For Redmic AI, how many questions you ask a day and how much text they took, to keep within its daily limit. Not the words themselves.
  • Which phone a dictation comes from: the free plan's weekly limit is per phone as well as per account, so a second account on the same phone doesn't get a second week. To count that, Redmic sends a number for the phone with each dictation, and our server keeps the week's count of free words under a scrambled form of it. On an iPhone, the number is a random one Redmic keeps in your iPhone's keychain. It isn't your phone's serial number or advertising identifier, no other app can read it, and it's gone when the iPhone is erased. On an Android phone, the number is worked out from the Android ID, a number Android gives Redmic on that phone, scrambled with SHA-256; the Android ID itself never leaves the phone. It stays the same when you reinstall Redmic and changes when the phone is reset to its factory settings. On a Mac the number is worked out from the Mac's hardware identifier, scrambled; the identifier itself never leaves the Mac. We don't use the number to track you.
  • Whether your phone has had a free trial: Redmic Pro's free trial, and an invite's free month, is one per phone. On an iPhone, Redmic asks Apple's DeviceCheck service, through our server, whether this iPhone has had one, and marks it when it gets one. Apple keeps that mark for us. It doesn't tell us who you are or give us an identifier for your iPhone, and we don't keep the token the app sends to ask. On an Android phone, our server keeps the mark, under the scrambled form of the phone's number described above, and keeps it after accounts are deleted, so that the trial stays one per phone. Before a phone gets its trial, Redmic asks Google Play Integrity, through our server, whether the request comes from Redmic installed from Google Play on a genuine phone. Google's answer says only that, not who you are, and we don't keep it.
  • Purchases: if you subscribe to Redmic Pro, Apple or Google tells us which plan you bought and when it renews, expires or is refunded, so we can grant Pro. On Android, Redmic gives Google Play your account ID with the purchase, so that the subscription stays with your account. We never see your payment details.
  • Your answers during setup: what you plan to use Redmic for, how you heard about it, your languages and default style, your data-sharing choice and, if you ask for the link to Redmic for Mac and PC, the email address to send it to.
  • The emails we send you: a welcome when you create your account, a few tips in your first two weeks, one question about how likely you are to recommend Redmic, and a reminder the day before a free trial ends. To write them we use your first name, your plan, and how much you've dictated (words, and on how many days), never what you said. We record which emails we sent. Each has an Unsubscribe link; sign-in codes still come whenever you ask for one. If you answer the question, we keep your score and anything you write with it.
  • Feedback and support messages: what you write to us, with the app version, iOS or Android version, device model and language, to look into the problem. Each message is also emailed to us, with your account's email address so we can reply. When you rate a dictation and have turned on Help improve Redmic, the rating can include that dictation's text.

We don't track you across apps and websites, show ads, or sell or rent your data. Redmic has no advertising, analytics or crash-reporting software in it.

Who processes it for us

  • Supabase hosts our server, database and sign-in, and the recordings kept for Help improve Redmic, in the European Union (Frankfurt).
  • Google Cloud hosts the part of our server that streams dictations to Soniox, in the European Union (Frankfurt).
  • Soniox transcribes Word-to-Word dictations while they're spoken.
  • OpenAI transcribes the other dictations.
  • Anthropic tidies and translates the text of dictations, finds the requests in it, and splits text pasted into notes into paragraphs.
  • Apple handles Sign in with Apple, App Store purchases and the iPhone's free-trial check (DeviceCheck).
  • Google handles Sign in with Google and, on Android, Google Play purchases (Google Play Billing), the free-trial check (Play Integrity) and the invite code passed on when you install Redmic.
  • Resend sends our emails: sign-in codes, the emails described above, and your support messages to us.

Each processes data only to provide its service to us. Apple and Google also handle your payments themselves, under their own terms and privacy policies.

How long we keep it

We keep your account data until you delete your account. The recordings kept for Help improve Redmic stay until you turn it off or delete your account; either deletes them all. Deleting your account (Menu › Account › Delete account, or without the app, as our account deletion page explains) erases your account, library, recordings, usage and purchase records from our server, and, when you delete it in Redmic on an iPhone, revokes Sign in with Apple. These stay:

  • The records of what your dictations cost us to serve, no longer tied to your account.
  • For 30 days, a scrambled fingerprint of the sign-in you used (not your email address or your Apple or Google ID itself), with how many words you had dictated that week and whether your free trial and invite month were used. It is there so that deleting an account and making a new one can't reset the free plan: if you sign up again within those 30 days, you carry on where you left off. After 30 days it is erased. You can ask us to erase it sooner.
  • The records kept for a phone rather than an account: the week's count of free words, erased within five weeks of the phone's last dictation on the free plan, and on Android the mark that the phone has had its free trial, kept while Redmic offers one.

Signing out deletes Redmic's data from that iPhone; on Android, uninstalling Redmic deletes it from the phone. Deleting the account doesn't cancel an App Store or Google Play subscription: manage an App Store one in Settings › Apple Account › Subscriptions, and a Google Play one in Google Play › Payments & subscriptions › Subscriptions.

Your rights

You can see, correct, download or delete your data. Most of it is in the app; for anything else, write to miralex13@gmail.com. Depending on where you live, you may also have the right to object to processing, and to complain to your data protection authority.

Children

Redmic isn't made for children under 13 (16 in the European Union), and we don't knowingly collect their data.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, we'll say so in the app before it takes effect.
